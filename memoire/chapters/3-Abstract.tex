\begin{otherlanguage}{english}

The integration of generative policies, such as Flow Matching, offers an effective framework for reactive and multimodal trajectory generation in robotic manipulation using visual inputs. However, learned generative models do not provide formal safety guarantees and may produce control commands that lead to collisions when operating in environments containing obstacles not seen during training. Conversely, reactive filtering methods based on Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) ensure formal forward invariance of safe sets, but traditionally rely on simplified geometric representations of the robot and its surroundings.

This thesis presents a decoupled two-layer architecture for the real-time, safe execution of trajectories generated by a frozen Flow Matching policy without requiring policy retraining. The proposed framework fully separates task planning from safety enforcement. The perception system combines a semantic segmentation model (SAM~3) with a persistent point cloud map to preserve obstacle representations during occlusions caused by wrist-mounted camera motions. The nominal policy generates joint-space velocity references at a rate of 3--5~Hz. A real-time reactive safety shield evaluates a Signed Distance Field (SDF) learned via Bernstein polynomials for the full robot body and solves associated CBF constraints at 100~Hz using Quadratic Programming (QP). This layer is complemented at 1~kHz by a reflex brake that dynamically adapts the certification horizon between solver iterations.

The architecture is evaluated in Gazebo simulation across two manipulation tasks (assisted feeding and pick-and-place) using both a Franka Emika Panda and a uFactory xArm7 manipulator. Experimental results show that the safety filter eliminates all 47 collisions observed under nominal policy execution across 60 unfiltered trials, while maintaining high task success (30/30 in pick-and-place) and inducing a mean tool deviation below 0.6~cm. Deployment on the second manipulator confirms the direct transferability of the safety shield by retraining only the link kinematics and distance fields. This work confirms that strict decoupling between generative policies and reactive filtering successfully reconciles learned policy flexibility with formal safety guarantees in robotic manipulation.

\end{otherlanguage}
